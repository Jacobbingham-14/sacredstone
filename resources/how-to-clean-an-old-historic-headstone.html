How Do You Clean an Old or Historic Headstone Safely?

Last updated July 2026 · Sacred Stone Headstone Cleaning

Quick answer

Older headstones — often marble, sandstone, or limestone — need the gentlest possible approach: plain water, the softest natural-bristle brush available, and a non-acidic biological cleaner applied slowly with patience. Skip anything abrasive entirely, and if a stone shows flaking, cracking, or looks unstable, it may need a conservation specialist rather than routine cleaning.

Why age changes the approach

Decades of weathering leave older stone more porous and structurally weaker than a modern granite marker, even when it looks solid. Materials common in older cemeteries, like marble and sandstone, are also more chemically reactive than granite, meaning products that are perfectly safe on a newer stone can still cause damage on an older one.

Signs a stone needs extra caution

  • Visible flaking or a sugary, granular texture on the surface
  • Hairline cracks or an existing repair
  • Lettering that's already worn shallow or hard to read
  • A base that looks tilted or unstable in the ground

Any of these are reasons to slow down and use the lightest possible touch — or, for a stone that seems structurally at risk, to consult a monument conservation specialist rather than proceeding with routine cleaning.

The gentlest version of the process

Wet the stone thoroughly first, apply a non-acidic biological cleaner and let it dwell far longer than you'd think necessary, then use only the softest brush available — natural bristle over nylon where possible. Rinse gently rather than with force, and let it air dry completely. Patience genuinely is the technique here; older stone rewards a slower process much more than a stronger one.
