Can You Use Bleach or Vinegar to Clean a Gravestone?

Last updated July 2026 · Sacred Stone Headstone Cleaning

Quick answer

No — avoid both. Bleach and vinegar are two of the most common DIY suggestions for headstone cleaning, but both can permanently damage stone. Bleach can discolor and weaken the surface over repeated use, and vinegar is acidic enough to etch marble and limestone, dulling detail and accelerating erosion.

Why these common suggestions are actually risky

Bleach and vinegar show up constantly in general "how to clean anything" advice because they're effective on things like mildew in a bathroom. Stone behaves very differently. Many headstones — particularly marble and limestone — are made of calcium-based minerals that react chemically with acids and strong oxidizers. What looks like a quick clean today can leave a chalky, roughened surface that shows up months later and only gets worse with each additional treatment.

What actually happens to the stone

Bleach can bleach out natural stone coloring unevenly and, over time, break down the surface enough that it becomes more porous — which ironically makes it collect dirt and biological growth faster afterward. Vinegar's acidity directly dissolves the calcium carbonate in marble and limestone, softening carved edges and inscriptions that are meant to last generations.

What to use instead

Plain water and a soft brush remove most everyday dirt safely. For algae, lichen, or moss, a conservation-grade cleaner like D/2 Biological Solution is designed to break down organic growth without the chemical risk that bleach and vinegar carry.

Sacred Stone never uses bleach, vinegar, or abrasive tools — only preservation-safe methods.
