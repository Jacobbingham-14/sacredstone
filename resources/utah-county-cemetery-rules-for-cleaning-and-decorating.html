What Are the Rules for Cleaning and Decorating Graves at Utah County Cemeteries?

Last updated July 2026 · Sacred Stone Headstone Cleaning

Quick answer

Utah County's municipal cemeteries — including Provo, Orem, Springville, and American Fork — generally allow family members to clean a headstone without a permit, since grounds and grass maintenance is already handled through a perpetual care fee paid at burial. Most also hold seasonal clean-up days for flowers and decorations, and private cemeteries may have their own separate policies.

What the cemetery already handles

A perpetual care fee, typically paid as part of the plot purchase, covers ongoing grass watering, mowing, and general grounds upkeep indefinitely. This is a common point of confusion — families sometimes assume "maintenance" includes the headstone itself, but it almost always refers to the surrounding grounds, not the marker.

Decoration and seasonal clean-up policies

Most municipal cemeteries schedule clean-up days, usually in early spring and again in fall, when flowers, wreaths, and other decorations are cleared from every plot as part of routine maintenance. It's worth checking with the specific cemetery office for exact dates if a decoration holds sentimental value you don't want removed.

Municipal vs. private cemeteries

Provo, Orem, Springville, and American Fork City Cemeteries are city-run and generally the most consistent in their policies. Private cemeteries like Eastlawn Memorial Hills and Timpanogos Memorial Gardens sometimes have their own separate rules, including preferred-vendor arrangements for commercial services, so it's worth a quick call to their office before assuming the same policies apply.
