How Do You Remove Lichen and Moss From a Headstone?

Last updated July 2026 · Sacred Stone Headstone Cleaning

Quick answer

Apply a non-ionic biological cleaner like D/2 Solution, let it dwell rather than scrubbing it off immediately, then gently loosen the growth with a soft brush and rinse. Lichen in particular has root-like structures that grip the stone surface, so heavy growth often needs a second treatment two to four weeks later rather than one aggressive scrub.

Why lichen is harder to remove than ordinary dirt

Lichen isn't just surface grime — it's a living organism that anchors itself into tiny pores and irregularities in the stone. Trying to scrape it off forcefully usually damages the stone surface before it fully removes the lichen, and can leave behind root fragments that regrow faster than before.

The patient approach that actually works

A biological cleaner works by breaking down the organism's cell structure over time, not by dissolving it instantly on contact. After application, it needs to sit — sometimes for days — while it does that work, ideally aided by natural rain and sunlight. Only after this dwell time should a soft brush be used to gently lift what's now loosened. For established colonies, a second round several weeks later removes what the first treatment couldn't fully release.

What to avoid

Wire brushes, putty knives, and pressure washers might look like they're working faster, but they typically roughen the stone surface in the process — which then gives future lichen spores more texture to grip onto, creating a worse cycle than before.
