How Do You Clean a Bronze Veteran or Military Grave Marker?

Last updated July 2026 · Sacred Stone Headstone Cleaning

Quick answer

Bronze markers need a dedicated bronze-safe cleaner, not a stone cleaner or biological solution — those can strip the protective oxidized patina that keeps the bronze from corroding. After cleaning, a thin protective wax is typically reapplied to preserve the marker's finish and slow future tarnishing.

Why bronze is a different job entirely from stone

Bronze markers, common for veteran and military graves as well as flush memorial plaques, aren't stone at all — they're metal, usually set into a granite or concrete base. Over time, bronze naturally develops a greenish-brown patina, which is actually a protective layer, not dirt. Removing it entirely with the wrong product exposes bare metal underneath to faster, more visible corrosion.

The safer cleaning approach

A bronze-specific cleaner, applied gently with a soft cloth rather than a stiff brush, lifts surface grime and light tarnish without stripping the patina down to bare metal. Once clean and fully dry, a thin coat of microcrystalline wax is commonly applied to protect the surface and slow future oxidation — a step stone markers don't need.

What to avoid on bronze specifically

Metal polishes designed for household fixtures, abrasive pads, and biological stone cleaners like D/2 are all a mismatch for bronze — some are too harsh, others simply aren't formulated for metal and won't address bronze-specific tarnish. If a bronze marker looks heavily corroded or has active green "bronze disease" spots, it's worth having it assessed by a bronze conservation specialist rather than cleaned routinely.
